# Flexibility for stiff adults

How the mobility, flexibility, hips, backbend and yoga branches start a stiff adult (can't touch the knees, sit cross-legged or squat flat-footed) and dose the work.

## Findings

1. **Any regular stretching works; type matters little.** Static and PNF (contract-relax) stretching gain more range than dynamic or ballistic. Neither volume, intensity nor frequency moderated gains across 77 studies. [Konrad 2024, JSHS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2023.06.002)
2. **Weekly time is the useful dose.** About 5 min per muscle group per week, spread over 5+ days, beat smaller doses. [Thomas 2018, Int J Sports Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29506306/)
3. **Strength training through full range gains as much range as stretching.** No difference between the two in 11 trials (n = 452). Loaded end-range work (Jefferson curl, Cossack, elephant walk, active holds) counts as flexibility work. [Afonso 2021, Healthcare](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33917036/)
4. **Early gains are mostly stretch tolerance.** Short programmes (under about 8 weeks) raise tolerance of the stretch sensation more than they lengthen tissue; structural change needs longer. Stretching that is mild and frequent works because it retrains the nervous system. [Weppler & Magnusson 2010, Phys Ther](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20075147/); [Freitas 2018, Scand J Med Sci Sports](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28801950/)
5. **Intensity need not hurt.** Mild stretches gain range comparable to hard ones. [Static stretching intensity review, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37092369/)
6. **Static stretching before strength work costs little.** Holds under 60 s per muscle have small to trivial effects on performance, removed by a dynamic warm-up. [Behm 2016, APNM](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26642915/); [Kay & Blazevich 2012, MSSE](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21659901/)
7. **Gains keep partly.** After 2–6 weeks off, range falls but stays above baseline. [Konrad 2025 detraining meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638565/)
8. **Timelines.** Pike and pancake usually come in months; front split and bridge take longer because hip flexors and spine adapt slowly (Merrick, below). Expect visible change in 4–8 weeks of consistent work.

## Principles we apply

- Every branch's first challenge is reachable by a very stiff adult: strap, cushion, wall, chair, blocks, towel under the heels, or holding a doorframe.
- Cues give the dose: mild pull (3–4 of 10, never sharp), 3×30 s or more, 3+ days a week; contract-relax as an option.
- Active and loaded work sits beside passive stretching: elephant walk, pike compression with bent knees, Jefferson curl from the floor, active strap hold.
- Body-capability tests have measurable rungs: deep squat, cross-legged sit, sit-to-rise, toe touch, overhead wall reach, knee-to-wall, dead hang.
- Check-in asks overall stiffness; "very stiff" grants nothing and focuses flex, mobility and hips.
