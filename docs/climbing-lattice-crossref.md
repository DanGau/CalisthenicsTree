# Climbing skills vs Lattice Training

Cross-check of [climbing-skills.md](climbing-skills.md) against Lattice Training. Lattice tests climbers at every level, so its data shows realistic progressions. Megos is an outlier.

## Summary

- **Finger numbers mostly fit.** `max_hang` (+10/+25/+40% BW) spans roughly V3–V8 on Lattice's data, and `max_hang_50` reaches V10–V11. Keep both.
- **Three challenge ladders are too hard for their tier:** `no_hang` (70% BW one hand is about V8), `repeaters` (6 sets of 7/3 at +10% is advanced), and `oa_hang_assist` (−5% BW is V12+). Proposed fixes are below.
- **Lattice tests a 7s hang. We use 10s.** A 10s max is about 3–5% lower than a 7s max. Keep 10s, but note the gap in the `hangboard` check-in.
- **Gates:** Lattice says start finger testing after at least 6 months of climbing and a 7s bodyweight hang on 20 mm. Campus is for adults only. Pulling strength caps power: if your 1-rep max pull-up is just bodyweight, you can't do campus moves. The doc's age and year gates are stricter than Lattice's, which is fine. Adding a pulling gate to campus would match Lattice.
- **Missing from the tree:** long feet-assisted "health" hangs (20–45s), a repeater endurance test (hang to failure at about 60% of max), a foot-raise / lateral-reach flexibility test, beginner floor core (Spider-Man holds, dish tucks), press-up and shoulder antagonists, and explosive pulls as a campus prerequisite.
- **Data gaps.** Lattice publishes no grade numbers for pull-ups, core, flexibility or endurance. It only says pull-ups roughly double across grades and press-ups barely change. Those numbers below are flagged as estimates.

## Lattice principles

- **Beginners** ([beginner's guide](https://latticetraining.com/blog/the-beginners-guide)): skill and climbing volume come first. Starting the hangboard too soon hurts recovery. For tendons, use long light hangs, such as 20s with one foot on the floor. Tendons adapt about 5× slower than muscle. Add pull-ups, press-ups, floor core (Spider-Man holds, dish tucks, moving planks) and hip mobility after climbing.
- **Start finger testing** after more than 6 months of climbing and a 7s bodyweight hang on 20 mm (same source). Lattice's remote testing targets climbers at V5 / f7a or above ([MP](https://www.mountainproject.com/v/113846618)).
- **Method by level** ([finger strength guide](https://latticetraining.com/blog/how-to-manage-finger-strength-for-climbers)): a lifting edge suits beginners and rehab, two-arm hangs suit intermediate to elite, and one-arm hangs are usually for elite climbers. Half crimp is the all-round grip. Train full crimp on the wall, not the board.
- **Hang durations:** max strength ≤10s, health 20–45s, repeaters 7s on / 3s off for 2+ reps.
- **Test:** 4–8 rising sets of 7s hangs with 2–3 min rest. Your max is the heaviest clean hang. Train at about 85% of max for 6×10s ([rung PDF](https://latticetraining.com/app/uploads/2018/06/1528799872597_180503_Lattice-Testing-Training-Rung-Instructions.pdf)).
- **Programming:** stick to 1–2 methods for 6–12 weeks, then retest. Train fingers twice a week, early in the session, never last. A sample plan runs 3 weeks on and 1 week deload, adding 1–3% load per week ([finger series](https://latticetraining.com/blog/training-series-finger-strength)).
- **Campus** ([Q&A](https://latticetraining.com/blog/video-qa-everything-you-need-to-know-about-campusing), [strength & power](https://latticetraining.com/blog/training-series-strength-power/)): adults only, since about 99% of juniors get a poor risk-to-reward ratio. Use it in a power phase 2–6 weeks before a goal, after a strength base. To go from 1-4-7 to 1-5-8 without a board, train one-arm hangs, deep lock-offs, heavy weighted pull-ups and explosive pulls ([power](https://latticetraining.com/blog/lattice-training-insights-training-power-applying-strength)).
- **Endurance** ([endurance series](https://latticetraining.com/blog/training-series-endurance)): 10×1 min on / 1 min off, about 2 grades below onsight. For regeneration, 3×5 min. ARC is 20–45 min. The self-test is a foot-on campus fatigue curve: work to failure, rest the same time, repeat 4–5 sets.
- **Antagonists and mobility:** Lattice finds press-ups barely correlate with grade, but keeps them in its plans for health. Its My Flex assessment benchmarks flexibility against similar climbers, but it publishes no numbers. In research, the lateral foot reach and an adapted Grant foot raise correlate weakly with grade (r ≈ 0.3) ([Draper 2009](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/24748668.2009.11868465)). Among elite climbers, hip abduction in the straddle sit and straddle stand correlated with grade ([PMC7178254](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7178254/)). That supports `active_straddle` and `straddle_sit`.

## Recommended additions

Ids are checked against `tree.ts` for clashes. Tiers and lanes are suggestions.

| id | name | branch | tier | lane | requires | what | cues | challenges |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| density_hang | Long Hangs | fingers | 2 | -1 | abrahangs | Lattice's tendon "health" hangs: 20–45s on 20 mm, one foot on the floor, at low effort. | Light load \| Steady breathing \| Half crimp | 4×20s \| 4×30s \| 4×45s, one foot down |
| max_test | Max Hang Test | fingers | 4 | 0 | hang_20 | Lattice's benchmark: work up through 4–8 sets of 7s hangs on 20 mm, resting 2–3 min. Record total load as % BW. Retest every 6–12 weeks. | Warm up fully \| Same grip every set \| Stop at a clean 7s | Test and log \| Retest after a block, +3% \| Retest +6% |
| repeater_test | Repeater Endurance Test | fingers | 6 | 1 | repeaters, max_test | 7/3 repeaters at 60% of your tested max, to failure. Lattice scores finger endurance this way. | Exact 7/3 timing \| Same grip \| Stop when form breaks | 12 reps (2 min) \| 18 reps (3 min) \| 24 reps (4 min) |
| campus_curve | Foot-on Campus Fatigue Curve | fingers | 5 | 2 | repeaters | Foot-on campus moves to failure, then rest the same time, for 5 sets. Lattice's aerobic self-test. | Feet on \| Smooth moves \| Time each set | 5 sets \| Last set ≥ 60% of first \| Last set ≥ 75% of first |
| explosive_pull | Explosive Pull-up | pull | 6 | 2 | pullup_12 | Pull fast enough that the hands leave the bar, or slap high. Lattice's campus substitute. | Drive from dead hang \| Hips still \| Soft catch | 3×3 chest to bar \| 3×3 hands off the bar \| 3×3 slap 30 cm above the bar |
| oa_scap | One-arm Scap Shrug | pull | 5 | 0 | scap_pull | Hang from one arm and pull the shoulder down without bending the elbow. The first step of Lattice's one-arm pull-up progression. | Straight arm \| Shoulder down \| Body still | 3×5 each \| 3×8 each \| 3×5 each, 3s hold |
| spiderman_hold | Spider-Man Hold | core | 1 | 1 | plank | Plank with one knee drawn up to the elbow, held. Lattice's beginner climbing core. | Hips level \| Knee to elbow \| Breathe | 15s each \| 30s each \| 45s each |
| foot_raise | Foot Raise Test | hips | 4 | 1 | high_step | Face a wall at arm's length. Raise one foot as high as you can and place it on the wall, keeping the hips square. An adapted Grant foot raise, which correlates with grade. | Hips to wall \| Standing leg straight \| Toe placed, not kicked | Foot to hip height \| Foot at mid-chest \| Foot at shoulder height, each leg |
| lateral_reach | Lateral Foot Reach | hips | 4 | 0 | wall_frog | Facing the wall, step a foot sideways as far as you can while the hips stay at the wall. A field test for turnout. | Hips to wall \| Turn the knee out \| Reach on the toe | 1 leg-length \| 1.2 leg-lengths \| 1.4 leg-lengths, each side |

Flag `foot_raise` and `lateral_reach` as uncertain: the research reports correlations, not grade targets. Set the challenge heights by feel.

Lattice also endorses **press-ups** as an antagonist; `pushup` already exists, so link it as a `requires` of `wrist_curls` or recommend it from the `fingers` focus. Its endurance sessions (10×1 min, ARC) need a wall, so they stay out of the tree.
